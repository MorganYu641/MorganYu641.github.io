Awards, Accolades, and Clubs

Black Belt in Taekwondo

I have worked hard over the better part of a decade to achieve Black Belt rank in Taekwondo, learning valuable lessons in patience, self-control, leadership, integrity, and more.

I learned that learning something, even something monumental, isn't hard, it's just about being consistent.

Eagle Scout

I have worked hard over the better part of a decade to become an Eagle Scout.

My Eagle Scout project was adding new signage to some of the Highland cemetary trails to help prevent people from getting lost.
The day that we did it regular trail goers were complementing us for the signage. Many people, even one of the volunteers, got lost before the signage was added.

During my Scouting career, I also went to Philmont. The trail we took at Philmont was two weeks of hiking and took us up Mount Baldy. We got there before the sunrise and it was gorgeous.

Robotics Club Founder

30501 is the ID for the Beechwood FTC Robotics team

The way that FTC Robotics works is for a year you and a team build a robot to complete a task in a challenge arena. The challenge that year was to make a robot that could pick up green and purple plastic balls off the floor and shoot them into a goal, robot basketball if you will. For each ball scored, a point was earned, however, you could earn bonus points for shooting them into the goal in a certain pattern of three balls that was established right before starting.

Formed in the 2025-2026 school year, we set our sights high and took upon ourselves the challenge of making a very ambitious robot with the following abilities:

  • Automatic aiming
  • Automatic tracking of the balls
  • Automatic sorting of the balls
  • Automatic loading
  • Automatic swapping between loading and firing
  • Autopilot

However, our ambition got the better of us and we couldn't get it fully functional within the bounds of the rules and had to learn compromise.

Archery 'almost 50' pin

For many years, I was in my school's archery team. I showed up regularly and practiced. In my final year I went to as many as five times per week. My efforts culminated in earning an 'almost 50 pin' which is a pin you could earn at certain tournaments for scoring a 49 out of 50. Many found the award belittling, but I was truely honored.
