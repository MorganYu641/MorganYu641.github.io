In The Pursuit of Perfection

Excellence of Outcomes

What is Excellence of Outcomes?

┕The act of producing your best works all the time.


For different people and for different projects, this can vary. For instance my first project in the Edge Showcase tab was a very complicated project but that didn't stop me from making it as good as possible. While it doesn't look very impressive on the outside, the inside is what counts. The innards are very intracate as result of ever expanding experience and scope of ideas as I worked on the project.


First came the men: I wanted the characters to be easily creatible allowing for creativity and diversity in characters. I did this by creating a base that was extendible, anyone creating a new character file only needs to call the each move's function and write any custom code.


Then came time: Most modern fighting games operate at 60 frames per second (FPS) and each move has some amount of frames for the time before a move hits, while a move can hit, and while the character is recovering from the attack for hit or whiffed moves. This means that the data for each characters actions are recorded each frame for 30 frames (0.5 seconds). This also allowed for directional inputs. Most modern fighting games also allow for directional inputs which means that a move can only be input by pressing a button after inputting down > down left > left (for instance) which allows for a greater diversity in move types.


Many more features came after and while I haven't made it pretty, I'm pursuing perfection in terms of teaching me as much as possible about programming while making something at the edge of my capacity, which I would call pretty Excellent Outcomes

Why Pursue Excellence of Outcomes?

┕Because it is fun.


"Why settle for mediocre when you could add more cool features?" is how I thought about it, I do personal projects not to pad out my resume, but to have fun and build something I am genuinely interested in. My fighting game credentials aren't too impressive: Tekken 8, DBFZ, Super Smash Bros Ultimate, Avatar Legends, Electoral Carnage, Melty Blood, Guilty Gear Strive, and a few other I've dabbled in, but I've enjoyed all of them at least a little so wanting to make something of my own was only natural.


When in Ceramics class I could've settled for the bare minimum, but instead I chose to go above and beyond. I'm not well versed in drawing, so the hardest and ultimately most plain looking pottery I could make were flat with only a painted design, to compensate I tried to make much more intracate designs into 3D, a canvas I could much easilier work.


In Intro to Computer Science I pushed myself to make a simple website design with a complicated centerpiece element. This was partially to push myself but, admittedly, partially out of spite. The professional website designer we were doing this with called buttons a complex element to work with, so I "one-upped" him by copying the most complex element in his website which was an element which had an image on one side, and buttons and text on the backside which spun when you moused over it.


This is all to prove that I Pursue Excellence no matter the occasion.
